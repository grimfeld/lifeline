# Life Level: fully derived progression, retroactive valuation, honest-quitting cascade deletion

The Life Level App (the life-level port, ADR 0001 pattern; fresh start, no data migration) stores only facts and derives all valuation. Three decisions travel together:

**Nothing numeric is stored.** A Habit carries only its Difficulty, a Negative activity only its Gravity and targeting; Raw XP, Level, decay and debt are computed at read from the Completion ledger plus the wall clock. All tuning constants (XP per level, decay grace/interval, damage tables) live in code, keyed by Difficulty/Gravity — the legacy per-row tuning columns from life-level's schema are dropped, forfeiting per-habit custom curves that were never exposed anyway. Consequence: a Level changes with no data change at all; anything displaying one computes on render.

**Edits re-value history.** Changing a Habit's Difficulty (or a Negative's Gravity) re-derives every past Completion at the new constants — a Level can jump or drop retroactively. Chosen over snapshotting the difficulty onto each Completion: Difficulty is the user's *current honest assessment* ("it was always this hard"), and a snapshot would be half-stale anyway unless decay params and level curves were frozen with it. The one thing that *is* persisted at log time is the random Negative's victim — that is a fact (a die roll's outcome), not a valuation, and must never be re-rolled by re-derivation.

**Deleting a Habit cascades.** Its own Completions die with it, and so do the Negative Completions that hit it — "honest quitting": earned levels *and* XP debt die together, and the debt lives in those Negative Completions. This knowingly erases part of a Negative's occurrence count; the alternative (keeping them with damage landing on nothing) was rejected to keep derivation total, with no dangling required Links. A fixed Negative whose rival Habit is deleted survives but is blocked from logging until re-paired or set to random — never silently switched to chaos.
