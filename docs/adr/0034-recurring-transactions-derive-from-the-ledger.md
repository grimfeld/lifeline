# Recurring transactions derive from the ledger

ADR 0033 made the Ledger ledger-first — Transactions are the facts, every total derives — but left one thing typed in twice: a **Recurring transaction** was a stored definition (payee, amount, Category, Account, rule, start day) *and* the Transactions it posted. The definition is redundant: rent, salary and Netflix are already written in the ledger, in the only handwriting that counts. So the definition goes. A **Recurring series** is now *derived*: one payee (normalized, so the bank's casing, punctuation and row digits collapse) whose Transactions keep a cadence — weekly, fortnightly, monthly, yearly — for near enough one amount. Three occurrences and a rhythm make a series; the run is read backwards from the newest Transaction, so a series describes what is still happening and a bill that changed day or price starts a fresh, shorter run instead of vanishing. A **Due** is that series projected one step past its newest occurrence: coverage is the ledger itself, so a day already paid can never be due and confirming a Due is nothing but posting the Transaction. Nothing about a series is stored. The two things that cannot derive — *muted* ("this regular payment is not a bill") and a waved-off due day — are one internal **Series override** Document, written only while it says something and deleted when it stops.

## Considered options

- **Keep the stored definition, offer detection as a "create from history" button** — rejected: the duplication survives, and with it the drift ADR 0033 spent a whole App avoiding. A definition that says €13.99 while the ledger says €15.99 is a second, worse truth about the same bill; deriving has no second truth to go stale.
- **Detect on the calendar alone (dates regular ⇒ a series)** — rejected: the weekly supermarket run is as regular as rent. Amount stability is what separates a bill from a habit, so the spread test does most of the work — a run whose amounts stray more than a quarter from the median is not a series at all.
- **Fuzzy payee grouping (the substring match auto-categorization uses)** — rejected here: grouping is transitive and a wrong merge is silent — two bills becoming one series, or a series with three payees. Exact normalized equality is predictable and explains itself; the payee's spelling is stable within one merchant, which is exactly the case that matters.
- **Keeping `recurringId` / `dueDate` on the Transaction** — rejected: they existed to mark which due a posting covered. Projecting from the newest occurrence answers the same question from the facts, so the fields are bookkeeping about bookkeeping. They are dropped from the type.
- **No stored state at all (drop mute and skip)** — rejected: a regular payment the user says is not a bill has no fact to derive from — it is intent, and intent must be stored or asked again every render. The override is the smallest possible home for it and disappears when it is empty.
- **A "series" Document written by a background sweep** — rejected: a cache of a pure function over the ledger, with every stale-cache problem and no benefit. Detection is a scan, and scans are fine at personal scale (ADR 0033's own precedent).

## Consequences

- The `ledger_recurring` type is retired; `ledger_series` (internal) replaces it, holding only `key`, `muted` and `skipped`. Existing `ledger_recurring` Documents are left in the store, inert and unregistered — the Transactions they posted are what the series now read, so nothing is lost.
- The Register's Recurring tab is a reading, not an editor: no "New recurring", no rule form. Per series it offers mute and a jump to those Transactions in the register; the Dues list keeps confirm (posts the Transaction) and skip (waves off one day).
- A series can be wrong the way any reading can: it appears only after three occurrences, and vanishes for two whole periods of silence (*dormant*), which is also how a cancelled subscription stops asking. The suggested amount is the run's median — a suggestion, never a promise, editable at confirm as before.
- Detection is O(transactions) per render, memoized on the ledger; it groups by payee and walks each group once.
- The forecast is unchanged in spirit (ADR 0033): the upcoming Dues *are* it. The overdue side needs no lookback window any more — dormancy ends a series on its own.
